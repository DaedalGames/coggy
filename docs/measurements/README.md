# Measurements

What the instrument found, newest first. Each record holds the numbers, the machine they came from, and the commit that produced them — so a figure quoted from here can be traced back to a run rather than to a memory. The findings above are ordered by what they decide instead, which is the view worth reading first.

## What M0 concluded

The findings, on 16 cores / 31 GiB / Windows 11, ordered by what they decide rather than when they arrived. Each links to the record that owns it; nothing here is stated that is not measured there.

1. **The agent CLI is the floor and the engine lands on it.** [The CLI holds 580 MiB for a session's whole life and averages 0.112 cores](2026-07-31-050322-the-agent-side-of-a-session.md), so a hundred sessions owe 58 GiB against a 22 GiB budget before an engine is opened — memory breaks at about 38 on the agent alone. The engine's cost is paid on top and only while it works: [between builds its own processes hold 8.97 MiB](2026-07-31-035111-between-builds.md), which is the shell rather than the session. [While building it takes 3.27 GiB](2026-07-31-035111-between-builds.md), and [only one session per engine installation can build at a time](2026-07-31-034150-unreal-builds-serialise.md), so scheduling cannot change that number. [Cooking is both heavy and concurrent](2026-07-31-040348-cooking-is-the-governed-state.md) — 5.02 GiB at peak, two editors side by side, and a hundred of them wanting 193 GiB against a 22 GiB budget. It [takes the build lock on the way in](2026-07-31-042802-the-lock-reaches-cooking-too.md) and runs freely past it, so two sessions alternate through and ten queue. **The ceiling for a cooking session is [nine](2026-07-31-054657-the-driven-duty.md) — [twelve on the engine alone, give or take 6.4%](2026-07-31-045604-an-error-bar-for-the-engine.md), less the agent holding the budget down with it.** [Concurrent cooks sum to their steady figure rather than their peak](2026-07-31-043156-cook-peaks-scatter.md), so the governor counts sessions rather than scheduling them apart; [that figure is the editor loading itself rather than the content](2026-07-31-043951-the-editor-is-the-cost.md), so eleven holds for any project the editor still dominates; and [a Defender exclusion does not move the ceiling either](2026-07-31-041923-what-an-exclusion-buys-a-cook.md) — it is memory, and an exclusion returns CPU.

2. **For sessions that compute continuously, cores bind and the ceiling is `redline = 2ηC/d`** — inversely with the cores a session demands, proportionally with the machine's logical processors. [Derived rather than fitted](2026-07-30-154348-duty-is-derivable.md), and checked against a rung predicted before it was run. `d` above 1 is ordinary: [an agent turn's tool work takes 2.63 cores](2026-07-31-015246-what-a-session-costs.md) while it builds. **`C` is the weak term**: this machine's sixteen cores [differ by 2.1× under load](2026-07-31-145412-the-cores-are-not-interchangeable.md), so one number stands in for three tiers. The relation itself now [holds to 9.9% at a duty 2.8× below where it was built](2026-08-01-080158-the-relation-at-a-quarter-duty.md), reading low, which is the conservative direction for admission, and `η` [does not fall between twenty-seven sessions awake and forty-five](2026-08-02-194155-eta-is-flat-where-it-was-said-to-fall.md) — so a fixed value carries above saturation. **How much of it is core placement is now bounded rather than open**: if the tiers set it, fresh solo holds would cluster in groups two-fold apart, and [three of the day's four solo sets spread 3.9–8.4% with no clustering at all](2026-08-02-221853-the-noisy-baseline-was-one-noisy-afternoon.md). The fourth spread 28.5% and is the one run whose background collapsed while it ran.

   **What `η` follows is the product `N·d`** — the number of sessions awake at any instant. A hundred sessions at duty 0.27 and a hundred and thirty-five at 0.20 leave twenty-seven awake either way, and [their total throughputs agree to within 1.5% while the session count differs by 35% and the duty by 26%](2026-08-02-121359-eta-follows-the-awake-count.md). So a hundred quiet sessions and twenty-seven busy ones are the same load, and one number decides admission. **But `η` also belongs to the workload**, so a ceiling without a footprint beside it is not a ceiling: [four values in these records split into 0.73–0.78 at 20 MiB resident and 0.84–0.93 at 80](2026-08-02-202535-the-core-ceiling-is-four-numbers-for-two-workloads.md), putting the same ceiling at 87 or at 109.6. **The split had the direction right and the size wrong by a factor of twenty, and never had the evidence for either**: those four runs varied duty as well as footprint and one of them fits a redline from a ladder rather than reading a slowdown at a hundred. Measured properly, [on undisturbed intervals `η` is the same at 20, 33 and 36 MiB](2026-08-03-024222-the-footprint-never-mattered.md) and the footprint is worth about 2% — the direction the split proposed, at a twentieth of the size, once one run's interruptions are taken out of its mean.

   **And `η` as this project computes it is two quantities multiplied.** It divides by the machine's core count, and the sessions never had it: three holds' own samples put the job at 14.11, 15.33 and 15.27 of sixteen. Dividing instead by what they held separates an occupancy term, which belongs to whatever else was running, from an efficiency term that belongs to the sessions — [and units per core-second is a route to the second that does not go through a solo baseline](2026-08-03-023657-eta-was-two-quantities-multiplied.md), which is the first `η` route that is not algebra on the others. **A governor admitting against `η` is admitting against a number that moves when something unrelated wakes up.** The machine's own state moves the same figure further than the weight does: at 20 MiB it reads 0.817 on a rested box and 0.426 on a slowed one, where three footprints on one rested box agree within 1.5%.

3. **A workload decides which condition you find.** Twenty megabytes of synthetic session put memory three orders clear and made cores the answer; [1.69 GiB of compiling Unreal](2026-07-31-022200-an-unreal-session.md) put memory first at thirteen sessions; nine megabytes of idle Unreal put it clear again. All three are correct about what they measured, and only the third is what a session mostly is.

4. **None of the four costs this project was scoped around is one.** Processes are cheap in memory — [dropping conhost returns 3.9% of the budget at a hundred sessions](2026-07-30-120002-first-redlines.md#what-dropping-conhost-is-worth-at-any-session-weight). Defender was [overstated by two orders of magnitude](2026-07-30-142218-defender-at-scale.md). Output has [three to four orders of headroom](2026-07-30-145414-output-path.md). [PLAN's own escape clause fired](../PLAN.md#residency-not-spawning) on that result.

5. **The engine ceiling is twelve sessions, ± 6.4%, held to what an RSS ceiling can be held to — and nine once the agent is added.** Five readings of a cooking session give [1.87 GiB a session](2026-07-31-045604-an-error-bar-for-the-engine.md) and the last carries a drift control that held at +0.8%. There is no slope to fit when total memory is the per-session figure times the count, so repeats and that control are [the whole of this condition's rigour](../../ROADMAP.md#current-priority-m0--attribution) — and neither needed the 22 GiB a ramp would have. The engine is only half a session, though, and [putting the CLI that drives it on the same budget gives 8.8 to 9.7](2026-07-31-054657-the-driven-duty.md).

6. **A redline from one ladder is worth ±12.5%**, from rungs that each reproduce within 2%. [The search was the noisy part rather than the measuring](2026-07-30-164912-redline-reproducibility.md), and solving the budget on a fitted slope brings the same seven runs to 2.3%. The fitted slope is `η`, which is memory contention between sessions rather than scheduling: ten sessions received every core they asked for, ran a fifth slower anyway, and left six idle doing it. **Priced across a range, that loss has no floor to start from** — [eight to twenty-eight sessions lose 34% per session while demanding under three quarters of the free cores](2026-08-02-220514-there-is-no-knee.md), with the marginal return of four more falling from 37.9 units/s to 3.1. So there is no count below which sessions are free of each other, and [what a governor can offer is a price rather than a ceiling](../../ROADMAP.md#m3--resource-governor).

7. **A ramp changes the machine it measures, and so does the hour it runs in.** A rung ran 4.8% slower at the end of one ladder than at its start, dragging the fitted redline down 3.9%, so every ramp repeats a rung as a control — and [the rule that follows](../../CLAUDE.md) is to do nothing else on the box while one runs. **That control covers one ladder and most claims here span two.** [A pseudoconsole ramp nine hours after its pipes counterpart returned 14 against 27 with both drift checks clean](2026-07-31-151821-the-pseudoconsole-row.md), because both machines held still and were not the same machine. Ramps now also repeat their solo rung, which is the fingerprint `sessionbench compare` uses to refuse a pair that cannot be subtracted.

   **Four scales, and only the smallest supports a comparison.** [The power state is worth 7.8×](2026-08-02-195840-the-same-command-on-battery.md) — a hundred sessions returned 907 units/s on mains and 135 on battery with every other recorded field identical, and no artifact carried the difference until one was made to. Run against run on one power state is worth up to 14%; hold against hold inside a run is 0.4–1.5%. The largest hid for a day because a figure carrying it does not read as a noisy version of the other state, it reads as a measurement of something else. **The second is thermal, and nothing reports it.** This box runs a solo session at about **18.9** rested and at **8.997 across twenty holds spanning 340 minutes** in a slower state, each level flat across its own samples, which is [72% on a slowdown](2026-08-03-003443-the-footprint-result-was-the-machine.md) — larger than anything the daemon or the workload does. It is not orderable: deliberate bursts of three and twenty minutes both failed, and so did 150 seconds of a hundred saturating sessions, after which the box read 20.295 nineteen minutes later. **The counters were tested at the next occurrence and both failed.** `thermal_c` and `processor_performance` do not move with it; what names the state is the workload's own rate, and what separates it from a crowd is [the cores held outside the job, which every hold now reports](2026-08-03-094550-the-slow-state-caught-on-a-quiet-machine.md) — at 1.03 cores held the machine still ran 9.608 against a rested 18.9, so a neighbour explains none of the halving. **Nothing has yet watched this state end.**

   **A third party can also take the machine mid-run, and a mean hides it.** Of three twenty-minute holds, one [lost 1.173 cores of a 15.39 median across two multi-minute episodes](2026-08-03-024222-the-footprint-never-mattered.md) where the others lost 0.061 and 0.052 — enough to read as an 11% workload effect that was not there. Holds and rungs now report median, mean and lost cores; **read the median.**

   **And the median is a fact about the job, where the question is about everything else.** Every sample now carries the whole machine's CPU on the job's own scale — it shipped on a sixteenth of it, so [the job read as larger than the machine in 31 of 36 samples](2026-08-03-061911-the-machine-column-was-narrower-than-the-job.md) and nothing objected, because the subtraction it exists for had not been done. Read as `(machine_cpu_percent - cpu_percent) / 100`, it separates the two ways a rate comes back low: a tenant leaves cores held, the slow state does not. **Three bands on this box at the gate's own workload — 18.9 rested, 9.0 slow, and tenanted a *band* of 11.95–17.10 (mean 14.741, n=48) where an earlier 13.8 came from nine holds — and a crowded rested box outruns a quiet slow one**, so no single reading names the state. [The tenanted figure's shape is now located](2026-08-11-103621-the-step-is-at-one-and-a-half-cores.md): a lone session steps 33% when something else crosses about 1.4 cores, with flat shelves either side, so 2 cores held and 12 read much the same and asking costs one 120-second solo hold rather than a `doctor` reading. **And the slow band is two states that a solo hold cannot separate at all.** [Solo holds agreeing to half a percent, 9.752 and 9.801, sat on boxes whose hundred sessions ran 246.4 and 902.8 units/s](2026-08-03-173452-the-slow-state-flatters-the-gate.md) — one crippled under load, one within 0.5% of its rested reference with only the lone session down. They push the gate's slowdown opposite ways, 3.958 against a 1.54 that *passes* a condition asking for 2. So a solo hold opens a window and cannot close this question: what separates them is a hundred-session hold's own total throughput, which splits **six runs between 903 and 1055 units/s from three between 217 and 288 with nothing in the 3.1× gap**, and which a hold now prints as `total`. **All nine were quiet, and the gap has since been filled from both sides** — 344.9 under a tenant, and 756.1 on the quietest box yet measured at 0.49 cores held — so the two clusters described ten readings rather than the machine. Read `total` beside the rest column, and treat a total far below ~900 as something being wrong rather than as a named state. **And it is mostly the neighbour**: across the seven hundred-session mains holds carrying a rest column, per-session rate against cores held elsewhere runs [r = −0.950 at slope −0.4889](2026-08-11-085752-the-neighbour-helps-one-session-and-robs-a-hundred.md), spanning the same 3.1× the clusters straddle — and **five of the seven sit at 8.5 cores held or more**, so "all nine were quiet" was said by an instrument that could not see tenancy. The same tenant moves a solo hold **+34% the other way**, so the slowdown compounds rather than cancelling, 1.255 both-quiet against 4.959 both-tenanted, with the 3.958/1.54 pair on the mixed cells.

   **And the machine has a limit on how long it can be measured at all**: [it stops dead at forty-one minutes of a hundred saturating sessions](2026-08-01-202112-gate-m1-at-twenty-minutes.md), no bugcheck, on mains, at 42 °C — where a hundred `ping` sessions held an hour the same morning, so it is the saturation rather than the count.

8. **A session's lifetime is not the lifetime of the process spawned for it, and that costs teardown rather than sessions.** Wrapping the same workload in `cmd` or `pwsh` [leaves the redline where it was](2026-07-31-141334-the-shell-costs-teardown.md) — 27, 26, 26 across three fitted crossings inside 3.6% — because a shell sleeps while its child computes. It costs 9 MiB and 70 MiB a session respectively, and **361× the teardown**: fifty wrapped sessions leave fifty stragglers, because killing the shell does not take its child. The same asymmetry [a pseudoconsole showed from the other direction](2026-07-30-101141-conhost-and-defender.md), and the thing a governor reclaiming a slot would get wrong.

One row of the comparison set stays open and is [named there rather than quietly dropped](2026-07-31-150258-g0-frozen.md). ## The pattern these share

**Every axis returned a wrong answer the first time it was actually exercised, and every wrong answer was a defect in the instrument rather than a fact about the machine.** Sampling that starved under load. A membership test that pid reuse turned into an undercount. A drop detector that counted a pseudoconsole's own startup sequences as lost output, and then — once that was fixed — counted a fast session's own counters against each other. A Defender cost that two workloads agreed on because both were measuring the same background noise.

A condition that has been passing is not a condition that works; it may only be a condition that has never been reached. That is the argument for pushing each axis until something breaks, and it is why these records are worth more than the numbers in them.

## Reading one

**A record's name and title carry the moment it was written, not the moment the run happened.** `YYYY-MM-DD-HHMMSS-<slug>.md`, taken from the commit that added it, so the directory sorts into the order the findings arrived and does so identically on any clone. Where a run predates its write-up the provenance table says so — one record here is stamped past midnight for measurements taken the previous afternoon.

Figures are what the run emitted, not what was typed up afterwards — `ramp.md` and `run.md` are generated. The two earliest records predate that and were transcribed by hand; both carry corrections for exactly the reason that practice stopped.

A redline quoted without its session count, workload, mode, and hardware is not quotable. Every headline here carries all four.
